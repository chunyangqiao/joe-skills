# Finishing details

Polish should reinforce the content and interaction that are already clear. A small, purposeful detail can give a surface character; extra decoration can compete with the task.

## States and empty content

Design relevant empty, loading, error, success, disabled, and populated states alongside the normal state. An empty state may explain what belongs here and offer a useful next step when one exists. Supporting controls that cannot do anything in an empty state may be hidden or disabled when that better matches the product; missing content alone is not a defect.

`ui.empty-state` is a prompt to consider the empty state as part of a feature, not an instruction that every surface must have one. Report it only when the actual flow needs an explanation or useful next step and its absence harms the user.

## Accents and backgrounds

Use accents, patterns, or subtle background changes to direct attention or distinguish a region. Reuse the product palette and check that decorative effects do not obscure text or status. Do not add artwork or borders just to make a sparse screen look busy.

`ui.background` covers background colors and gradients. Start with the established palette and nearby hues to keep a background coherent with the content. An unplanned cross-family gradient can introduce a competing palette; check it against the brief and brand. Deliberate multicolor artwork is a valid design direction when the product calls for it.

## Final pass

Check alignment, wrapping, truncation, hover/focus/pressed feedback, and consistency between related components. Review the rendered page at its intended widths. Keep only details that help recognition, hierarchy, comprehension, or interaction.
