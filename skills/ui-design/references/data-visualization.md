# Data visualization and data-heavy UI

Choose a display that makes the intended comparison easy. A chart is not automatically clearer than a number or table.

- Use bars for category comparisons, lines for change over time, distributions for spread, and tables when precise values or cross-row lookup matter. Treat chart selection as a fit to the question and data, not a fixed mapping.
- Label values and series directly when that reduces legend lookup. Order categories meaningfully and make axes and units clear.
- Check whether the scale or baseline could mislead. A zero baseline is often important for bars; other charts may need a different scale to show a meaningful pattern. State the choice clearly.
- Use a limited, consistent palette. Do not encode series or status by hue alone; add labels, patterns, line styles, or shapes as appropriate.
- Include a readable text or tabular alternative when the visualization carries important information. Check keyboard access and screen-reader semantics for interactive charts.
- Review dense tables at narrow widths; preserve the ability to compare labels and values.

Avoid universal limits such as a fixed number of acceptable bars or lines. Readability depends on labels, scale, data, and interaction.
