# Design brief and exploration

Use a small brief to make the visual choices legible before implementation. Do not turn this into a required questionnaire when the task is already clear.

## Brief shape

Record only what affects the interface:

- **Audience and task:** who is using the surface and what they need to accomplish.
- **Content hierarchy:** primary, supporting, and contextual information; identify the main action when one exists.
- **Character:** a few concrete adjectives grounded in the product and audience. Translate these into choices such as type, color, density, shape, and language.
- **Constraints:** existing components and tokens, required states, responsive behavior, accessibility needs, and supplied assets.
- **Open choices:** decisions the user has delegated; make a coherent choice and note its rationale.

## Explore the feature

Start with the actual feature and its content. A small working slice often reveals layout and state questions more clearly than a detailed abstract mockup. Keep early exploration cheap, and postpone polish until structure and interactions make sense. For a larger surface, iterate one useful slice at a time and learn from the rendered result.

Use grayscale as an optional way to diagnose whether structure and hierarchy work without color. It is a design technique, not a mandatory implementation stage; a deliberate high-fidelity exploration is also valid.

## Product personality

Type, palette, shape, density, imagery, and wording all contribute to the product's character. Choose them as a coordinated system. Treat common associations (for example, blue as familiar or rounded shapes as friendly) as hypotheses to test against the product, not fixed meanings.

`ui.brand-color` anchors the palette decision: inspect the established brand before choosing a primary color. A new hue that displaces an existing brand without a deliberate design decision is system drift; a requested rebrand is a separate, legitimate task.
